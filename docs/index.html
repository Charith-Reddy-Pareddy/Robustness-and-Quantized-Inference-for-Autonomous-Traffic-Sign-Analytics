Traffic-sign recognition · robustness & quantization

Does compressing the model compromise what it sees?

View source on GitHub →

Two GTSRB traffic-sign classifiers — a from-scratch CNN and a fine-tuned MobileNetV2 — trained across 5 seeds, quantized to INT8, and stress-tested against corruption, adversarial attacks, and a second dataset. INT8 preserves accuracy and cuts latency ~2x and size ~3.4x — but whether it's genuinely as robust depends on which threat model you ask about, and the two most-quoted numbers in this kind of report turn out to need real qualification. Full methodology in ROBUSTNESS_REPORT.md.

With uncertainty: at severity 4, MobileNetV2 brightness/contrast accuracy is 75.2% [71.6, 78.8] FP32 vs. 66.7% [62.1, 71.2] INT8 (95% CI, 5 seeds) — a −8.5pp mean drop [−9.3, −7.7] bootstrap CI, paired t p<0.0001, Cohen's dz = −8.2. This is a real, well-separated effect, not noise.

Out-of-distribution generalization

Same 23 classes, evaluated on GTSRB (dashcam, Germany) vs. Mapillary+DFG (street-level, different country/signage). The FP32-vs-INT8 gap barely moves — the real story is architecture, not quantization.

05

The transfer-attack caveat, quantified

§3's "INT8 transfer ≤ white-box" finding attacks the quantized model with examples crafted against its own near-identical FP32 twin. A genuine black-box attack — crafted against the other, independently-trained architecture — tells a very different story.

At ε=1/255, same-architecture INT8 transfer succeeds 46–85% of the time; a genuine cross-architecture black-box attack succeeds just 0.5–0.9% of the time. The high "transfer" numbers in §3 mostly measure two nearly-identical decision boundaries, not something intrinsic to INT8.
06

Quantization-aware training (QAT)

A third variant alongside FP32 and post-training static quantization (PTQ): fine-tuned with fake-quantization for 3 epochs from the converged FP32 checkpoint. evaluation in progress

QAT training is complete for both architectures. Full corruption and adversarial re-evaluation (including a true INT8 white-box attack via QAT's differentiable fake-quant surrogate) is still running — this section will update once it lands. Early clean-accuracy read: baseline CNN improved to 95.0% (from 91.8% FP32/91.8% PTQ) after 3 extra fine-tuning epochs; MobileNetV2 dropped to 95.3% (from 97.2% FP32/96.6% PTQ) — QAT numbers are confounded with those extra epochs, which PTQ never received.
